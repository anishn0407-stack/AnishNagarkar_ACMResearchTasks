Review: "Prompt Engineering Techniques: Streamlining 

AI Workflows for Academic Deadlines" by Gauri Shirke 

This article tackles prompt engineering through the relatable problem of a stressed 

college student trying to get useful help from an AI assistant at 2:30 AM. Rather than 

opening with dry technical definitions, the piece frames prompting as something we 

already do naturally the same way we rephrase a question for a friend to be better 

understood and then builds outward into a structured toolkit. 

The article breaks prompting down into five core building blocks (role, task, instructions, 

context, input) before moving through techniques of increasing complexity: zero-shot, one

shot, and few-shot prompting for quick or pattern-based requests, followed by advanced 

methods like Chain of Thought, Role-based, Contextual, Retrieval-Augmented, and Tree of 

Thought prompting. Each technique comes with a practical, assignment-related example 

which was a nice touch — it bridges the conceptual explanation with something actually 

usable. 

The two techniques that stood out most to me were Chain of Thought and Contextual 

prompting, mainly because I end up using both almost every time I work with AI. Chain of 

Thought is my go-to whenever I need to actually think through a problem instead of getting 

a final answer handed to me forcing the AI to lay out its reasoning step by step makes it 

way easier to spot where the logic breaks or where I need to redirect it. Generally, I use it 

whenever I am trying to learn something through a problem of DSA or CP. Contextual 

prompting is the one I probably rely on even more, honestly, since without 

background details the responses always feel generic. The moment I add context like 

deadlines, constraints, what I've already tried, the answers get noticeably sharper. 

Reading this made me realize I'd been doing this instinctively without knowing there 

was a name for it. I also use this for keeping a track on my progress in whichever field 

I want to. 

This connects to broader conversations around AI literacy right now as more students rely 

on LLMs daily. Knowing how to structure a request is becoming as important as knowing 

what to ask. The article's closing insight that refining how you prompt AI also sharpens how 

you communicate with people is a subtle but effective takeaway that elevates it beyond a 

simple technique list.
